Stop Waiting Until You're Ready

Sometimes readiness doesn't come before the beginning. Sometimes the beginning is what creates it.

Brian Volkmuth  •  3 min read  •  Growth

I've postponed things because I wasn't ready.

At least that's what I told myself.

I needed more information. More experience. More time to think. A better plan. Maybe a little more confidence.

Some of that was legitimate.

Preparation matters.

But if I'm honest, there have also been times when “I'm not ready” sounded more responsible than “I'm afraid to start.”

That's an uncomfortable distinction.

Ready for what?

The strange thing about readiness is that I'm not sure we ever define it.

What exactly am I waiting for?

To know everything I might need to know? To feel completely confident? To eliminate the possibility of making the wrong decision? To know how the story ends before I agree to begin it?

I've waited for some version of all of those things.

And I've learned that life doesn't often provide them.

There wasn't a moment when I suddenly knew I was ready to be a husband or father. There wasn't a morning when I woke up completely prepared to lead other people. Going back to school didn't begin with certainty that I could finish. Teaching didn't start after I'd learned everything I would ever need to know.

Some responsibilities teach us how to carry them only after we've picked them up.

Preparation still matters

I don't want to confuse starting before we're completely ready with starting carelessly.

There are things we should learn first. Questions we should ask. People we should listen to. Consequences we should consider.

If I'm about to make a decision that affects my family, finances, health, or other people who trust me, “I'll figure it out” isn't always an adequate plan.

Readiness isn't all or nothing.

Am I missing something I genuinely need before I begin, or am I waiting for uncertainty to disappear?

Those are different problems.

The first calls for more preparation.

The second may require something preparation can't provide.

A decision.

Beginning changes what we know

Before we start, much of what concerns us is theoretical.

What if this happens?

What if I can't handle that?

What if I've overlooked something?

Then we begin.

Reality replaces some of the speculation. We encounter the actual problem instead of every problem we imagined. We learn what we got right. We discover what we misunderstood. We adjust.

That doesn't mean everything works out. Sometimes beginning reveals that we made the wrong choice.

But even then, we're working with information we couldn't have gained by standing still.

There is a point where more waiting stops making us more ready.

It simply keeps us from beginning.

Ready enough

I'm trying to become more comfortable with those two words:

Ready enough.

Not careless. Not unprepared. Not certain everything will work.

Ready enough to take the next responsible step.

Maybe the entire path isn't clear. Maybe there are still boxes to unpack, things to learn, conversations to have, and parts of ourselves that have to catch up with the decision we've already made.

That's okay.

The goal isn't to have everything figured out before taking the first step.

Sometimes the first step is how we begin figuring it out.

You may never feel completely ready for what comes next. Sometimes you become ready by going.
